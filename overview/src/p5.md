P5 tests owned hardware against the physical adversary of P1's tier 2 (assumption A-08): tamper-evident seals over the FC's SD card, debug connectors and wiring, and an FC debug port that cannot be used in the flight configuration (P1 `README.md`; `docs/08-verification-plan.md`). P5 does not need SITL. It owns {{p1.repo_reqs:P5}} requirements.

**Inherited from P2.**

- **Physical attacks on the boot chain and the TPM.** P2 places debug ports, bus interposers and glitching out of its scope and in P5's (P2 `docs/01-scope-and-trace.md` §1.5). Neither CI nor P2's hardware runs can prove resistance to TPM bus interposers, a TPM reset, debug-port access or fault injection (P2 `docs/06-verification.md` §6.2).
- **TPM reset and replay (A2-02).** SR-010.4's encrypted session stops passive reads of the unsealed keys, not an attacker who can reset the TPM on its own and replay approved measurements. P2 assumes the TPM resets only at power-on, with its reset pin on the module's pull-up (DD2-17), to be checked on the module bought; P1 records the remaining risk in THR-034, and it is P5's scope (P2 `docs/03-measured-boot-sealing.md` §3.6; P1 `docs/05-threat-model.md` §5.5).
- **The FC boot loader's root of trust.** The PX4 boot loader sets no flash write-protection, so SR-018 holds only while the debug port is disabled or sealed, which is P5's SR-024 (P2 `docs/04-fc-secure-boot.md` §4.1; SR-018's rationale in P1 `data/requirements.csv`).
